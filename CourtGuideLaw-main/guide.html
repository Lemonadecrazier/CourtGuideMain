Who Is Who?

Court cases can involve many different people. Hover over a role to learn more about what they generally do in a court case.

🛡️

Defendant

The person or party responding to a legal claim or accusation.

A defendant may need to respond to court documents, attend hearings, present evidence, and follow important court deadlines.
Learn about defendants →
📄

Plaintiff

The person or party who generally brings a civil claim against another party.

A plaintiff generally starts a civil case by making a legal claim and may need to provide evidence supporting that claim.
Learn about plaintiffs →
👁️

Witness

A person who may provide information about events or facts relevant to a case.

A witness may be asked questions about what they personally saw, heard, experienced, or otherwise know about the case.
Learn about witnesses →
⚖️

Judge

A judicial officer who handles matters brought before the court.

A judge may manage court proceedings, consider evidence and arguments, interpret applicable law, and issue decisions or orders.
Learn about judges →
👨‍⚖️

Lawyer

A legal professional who may represent or advise someone involved in a case.

A lawyer may explain legal options, prepare documents, communicate with other parties, and represent a client during proceedings.

🏛️

Court

The institution where legal proceedings are heard and decided.

Courts provide the formal setting for legal proceedings, manage cases and hearings, and may issue judgments, orders, or other decisions.

Defendant Guide

A defendant is generally the person or party responding to a legal claim or accusation.

📄

Read Your Documents

Carefully review summonses, complaints, charges, notices, and other court documents.

Review your court papers carefully. They can explain what the case is about, what you may need to do, and when action may be required.
📅

Know Your Deadlines

Check your official documents for response deadlines, hearings, and important dates.

Write important dates on a calendar and check your official documents regularly so that you do not overlook a required response or hearing.
Organize Here →
📁

Organize Evidence

Keep relevant documents, photographs, messages, and records organized.

Keep important evidence together and label files clearly. Organized records can make it easier to locate information when needed.
🏛️

Attend Court

Follow the instructions in your official court documents and arrive prepared.

Check the correct date, time, location, and any instructions provided by the court. Allow extra time for travel and court procedures.
⚠️

Don't Ignore Notices

Court notices may contain important deadlines or instructions.

Read notices as soon as you receive them. If you are unsure what a notice means, consider contacting the court or a qualified legal professional for appropriate guidance.
✓

Stay Organized

Keep copies of documents, evidence, correspondence, and court orders together.

Create a folder for your case and keep important paperwork in date order. Keep copies of anything you submit or receive.

Plaintiff Guide

A plaintiff is generally the person or party who brings a civil claim against another party.

📝

Understand Your Claim

Clearly identify what happened, who is involved, and what outcome you are seeking.

Identify the important facts, the parties involved, and the result or remedy you are asking the court to consider.
📁

Gather Evidence

Organize documents, photographs, messages, records, and other relevant information.

Keep evidence that may support your claim organized and make copies of important documents for your records.
📋

Complete Forms

Complete required court forms accurately and follow applicable filing procedures.

Check forms carefully before filing and make sure you follow the court's instructions for submitting documents and paying any required fees.
📅

Track Your Dates

Keep track of filing deadlines, hearings, meetings, and other important dates.

Use a calendar or reminder system to keep track of every deadline and scheduled court appearance connected to your case.
🏛️

Prepare for Court

Review your documents beforehand and bring anything the court requires.

Review your important facts and documents before attending. Follow any instructions provided by the court about what to bring.
✓

Keep Records

Keep copies of filings, correspondence, evidence, and court orders.

Keeping a complete record can help you understand the history of your case and locate documents when they are needed.

Witness Guide

A witness may provide information about events or facts relevant to a case.

👁️

Know What You Know

Focus on what you personally saw, heard, experienced, or otherwise know.

Separate what you personally know from assumptions or information you heard from someone else. Answer based on your own knowledge.
🗣️

Tell the Truth

Give honest answers and do not intentionally change or exaggerate information.

Give accurate answers to questions. Do not intentionally exaggerate, hide, or change information when providing testimony.
❓

Listen Carefully

Listen to each question carefully before answering.

Take a moment to understand the question. If you do not understand what is being asked, you may need to ask for clarification.
🤔

Don't Guess

If you do not remember or do not know, say so rather than guessing.

It is generally better to explain that you do not remember or do not know than to provide an uncertain answer as if it were a fact.
🏛️

Follow Instructions

Follow instructions provided by the court regarding your appearance.

Pay attention to instructions about when and where you need to appear and any procedures you must follow as a witness.
⚠️

Handle Information Carefully

Follow any applicable restrictions about discussing testimony or case information.

Some cases may involve specific instructions about discussing testimony or case information. Follow instructions that apply to your situation.

Judge Guide

A judge is a judicial officer responsible for handling matters brought before the court and applying the law and court procedures.

⚖️

Hear the Case

A judge may listen to arguments, testimony, and evidence presented during proceedings.

During proceedings, the judge may listen to the parties, consider evidence, and address questions that arise during the case.
📜

Apply the Law

Judges apply relevant laws and procedural rules to matters before the court.

The judge considers the laws and court procedures that apply to the particular matter before the court.
📝

Make Decisions

A judge may issue orders, rulings, judgments, or other decisions depending on the case.

Decisions can address procedural questions, evidence, motions, or the final outcome, depending on the type and stage of the case.
🏛️

Manage Proceedings

Judges may manage hearings and help ensure proceedings follow applicable rules.

A judge may control the order of proceedings, address procedural issues, and help maintain an orderly and fair courtroom process.

Lawyer Guide

A lawyer is a legal professional who may advise or represent a person or organization in legal matters.

🗣️

Provide Legal Advice

A lawyer may explain legal issues, procedures, options, and considerations.

A lawyer can help a client understand how legal rules and procedures may apply and discuss possible options.
📄

Prepare Documents

Lawyers may prepare or review legal documents, filings, and other materials.

Depending on the matter, a lawyer may draft, review, organize, or submit documents required during the legal process.
🏛️

Represent Clients

Depending on the matter, a lawyer may speak on behalf of a client during proceedings.

When authorized to represent a client, a lawyer may communicate with other parties and speak for the client during proceedings.
🤝

Explain Options

A lawyer can help a client understand available legal options and considerations.

A lawyer may explain possible approaches, potential consequences, and considerations that could affect a client's decision.

Court Guide

A court is an institution where legal proceedings are heard and decisions are made according to applicable laws and procedures.

🏛️

Hear Cases

Courts provide a formal setting where legal disputes and proceedings can be heard.

Courts provide a structured environment where parties can present matters for consideration under applicable procedures.
📋

Manage Proceedings

Courts manage filings, hearings, documents, schedules, and other parts of proceedings.

Court administration can involve processing filings, scheduling hearings, maintaining records, and managing case information.
⚖️

Make Decisions

Courts may issue judgments, orders, or other decisions depending on the type of case.

A court's decision may resolve a dispute, determine a legal issue, or establish what the parties must do next.
📜

Follow Court Rules

Court proceedings are governed by applicable laws, rules, and procedures.

The specific rules depend on the jurisdiction and type of case. Official court information should be checked for applicable requirements.

Court Process

This is a simplified overview of steps that may occur in a court case. Actual procedures vary depending on the type of case and location.

📄

1. Filing

A case generally begins when appropriate documents are filed with the court.

The person or party starting the case generally submits required documents to the appropriate court according to applicable filing rules.
📬

2. Notice

The other party may receive formal notice of the case or claim.

Formal notice can inform another party that a case has been started and may explain what response or action is required.
📝

3. Response

Depending on the case, the responding party may have an opportunity to answer.

The responding party may have a specific period in which to submit an answer or another response, depending on local rules.
🔎

4. Preparation

Parties may gather documents, identify evidence, and prepare their arguments.

Preparation may include organizing records, identifying witnesses, reviewing evidence, and preparing arguments or legal documents.
🏛️

5. Hearing or Trial

The court may hear arguments, testimony, and evidence according to applicable rules.

The format and length of a hearing or trial can vary significantly. The court determines how the proceeding is conducted under applicable rules.
⚖️

6. Decision

The court may issue a judgment, order, or another decision.

The decision may resolve some or all issues in the case. Depending on the jurisdiction, further review or appeal may sometimes be available.

Important!

Court procedures can differ significantly depending on the jurisdiction and type of case. Always check official court information for the rules that apply to your situation.
